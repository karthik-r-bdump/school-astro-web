## Part I: Civics (30 Marks)

### Question 1 Solutions [10 Marks]
1. **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** [2 Marks]
2. Adopted: **26 November 1949**; Enacted: **26 January 1950** [2 Marks]
3. **Universal Adult Suffrage**: Every Indian citizen who is 18 years of age or above has the right to vote in elections regardless of gender, caste, religion, or wealth. [2 Marks]
4. Two Fundamental Duties:
   - To respect the National Flag and National Anthem. [1 Mark]
   - To safeguard public property and abjure violence. [1 Mark]
5. **Preamble**: An introductory statement in the Constitution that states its guiding philosophy, core objectives, and values. [2 Marks]

### Question 2 Solutions [20 Marks]
- (a) **Preamble Terms** [6 Marks]:
  - (i) Sovereign: Complete political independence from foreign rule; absolute authority over domestic and foreign affairs. [2 Marks]
  - (ii) Socialist: Equitable distribution of national wealth; welfare state reducing economic inequality. [2 Marks]
  - (iii) Secular: No state religion; all religions enjoy equal status, respect, and freedom of practice. [2 Marks]
- (b) **Fundamental Rights** [10 Marks]:
  - (i) Right to Equality: Guarantees equal treatment before the law. Provisions: Abolition of untouchability (Art 17); Abolition of titles (Art 18); Equal opportunity in public employment (Art 16). [4 Marks]
  - (ii) Right to Constitutional Remedies: Empowers citizens to approach the Supreme Court or High Courts via writs (Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, etc.) if their Fundamental Rights are violated. [3 Marks]
  - (iii) Right against Exploitation: Prohibits human trafficking, forced unpaid labor (*begar*), and employment of children below 14 years in hazardous industries. [3 Marks]
- (c) **Fundamental Rights vs Directive Principles** [4 Marks]:
  - Fundamental Rights are justiciable (legally enforceable in courts); Directive Principles are non-justiciable (guidelines for governance, cannot be enforced by court order). [2 Marks]
  - Rights establish political democracy; Principles aim at establishing social and economic democracy. [2 Marks]

### Question 4 Solutions [40 Marks]
#### Question 4.1 Solutions [10 Marks]
- (a) **Blood and Iron Policy**: Balban's ruthless policy to crush Mewati robbers, recalcitrant Rajput chiefs, and internal rebels through espionage, harsh corporal punishments, and public executions. [3 Marks]
- (b) **Chahalgani & Destruction**: The Forty (*Chahalgani*) were Turkish nobles created by Iltutmish who became kingmakers. Balban broke their power by eliminating powerful leaders through poison/execution and promoting loyal lower officers. [4 Marks]
- (c) **Iltutmish Achievements**: Saved the Sultanate from Mongol ruler Genghis Khan by refusing refuge to Jalal-ud-din; introduced standardized coinage (*Tanka* and *Jital*); completed the Qutb Minar. [3 Marks]

#### Question 4.2 Solutions [10 Marks]
- (a) **Market Control**: Fixed maximum retail prices for grains, textiles, and cattle; set up separate regulated markets (*Mandi*); appointed market controllers (*Shahna-i-Mandi*); banned hoarding and black-marketing. [4 Marks]
- (b) **Military Reforms**: First Sultan to maintain a permanent standing army paid in regular cash from the state treasury, rather than granting land assignments (*Iqtas*). [3 Marks]
- (c) **Mongol Threat**: Repaired and reinforced northwestern frontier forts; stationed seasoned generals; defeated multiple Mongol incursions decisively. [3 Marks]

#### Question 4.3 Solutions [10 Marks]
- (a) **Capital Transfer Failure**: Distance of 1500 km between Delhi and Daulatabad caused untold suffering and deaths during summer marches; lack of water at Daulatabad; northwestern border was left vulnerable to Mongol invasion. [4 Marks]
- (b) **Token Currency Failure**: Bronze and copper coins were easily forged because the royal mint did not use distinctive secret marks; citizens paid taxes in fake coins and hoarded silver/gold, bringing trade to a standstill. [4 Marks]
- (c) **Doab Taxation Consequences**: Taxes were raised during an acute famine; impoverished peasants abandoned their lands and rebelled; agriculture suffered severe destruction. [2 Marks]

#### Question 4.4 Solutions [10 Marks]
- (a) Founded by **Harihara and Bukka** in **1336 AD** under the guidance of sage Vidyaranya. [2 Marks]
- (b) **Krishnadeva Raya**: Great scholar and patron; composed *Amuktamalyada* in Telugu and *Jambavati Kalyanam* in Sanskrit; patronized the eight eminent poets (*Ashtadiggajas*), including Tenali Rama; built Hazara Rama and Vitthala temples. [4 Marks]
- (c) **Battle of Talikota (Rakshasi-Tangadi, 1565 AD)**: Combined armies of Deccan Sultanates (Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmednagar, Bidar) crushed Vijayanagar; the capital Hampi was plundered and ruined. [4 Marks]
