# Chapter 1: The Delhi Sultanate

## 🎯 Syllabus Scope & Objectives
- Five dynasties of the Delhi Sultanate: Mamluk/Slave (1206–1290), Khalji (1290–1320), Tughlaq (1320–1414), Sayyid (1414–1451), Lodi (1451–1526).
- Mamluk dynasty: Qutb-ud-din Aibak, Iltutmish (Forty nobles / Chahalgani), Razia Sultan (first woman ruler), Ghiyas-ud-din Balban (Blood and Iron policy, Sijdah and Paibos).
- Khalji dynasty: Alauddin Khalji (administrative reforms, market control, price regulation, branding of horses / Dagh, descriptive roll / Chehra).
- Tughlaq dynasty: Muhammad bin Tughlaq (shifting capital from Delhi to Daulatabad, token currency experiment, taxation in Doab).
- Architecture: Qutb Minar, Alai Darwaza.

---

## 📖 Key Concepts & Detailed Notes
### 1. The Mamluk (Slave) Dynasty (1206–1290)
- **Qutb-ud-din Aibak (1206–1210)**: Founder of the Sultanate; known as *Lakhbaksh* (giver of lakhs) due to his generosity. Started construction of the Qutb Minar.
- **Shams-ud-din Iltutmish (1211–1236)**: Real consolidator of the Sultanate. Organized the *Chahalgani* (The Forty Turkish nobles). Introduced the silver coin *Tanka* and copper coin *Jital*. Completed the Qutb Minar.
- **Razia Sultan (1236–1240)**: First and only female Muslim monarch of the Delhi Sultanate. Nominated by her father Iltutmish; discarded female attire and rode out in public. Faced severe opposition from conservative nobles.
- **Ghiyas-ud-din Balban (1266–1287)**: Broken the power of the Chahalgani. Propounded the 'Divine Right of Kings' (monarch is God's representative on earth, *Zill-i-Ilahi*). Implemented strict court decorum including *Sijdah* (prostration) and *Paibos* (kissing the Sultan's feet). Adopted the policy of **Blood and Iron** against rebels.

### 2. The Khalji Dynasty & Alauddin Khalji (1296–1316)
- **Market Control & Price Regulation**: Fixed prices of all essential commodities (grains, cloth, horses, slaves). Appointed market supervisors (*Shahna-i-Mandi*).
- **Military Reforms**: Paid soldiers in cash. Introduced **Dagh** (branding of royal horses to prevent substitution) and **Chehra** (descriptive roll of each soldier).
- **Southern Expeditions**: Dispatched general Malik Kafur to conquer the Deccan kingdoms (Yadavas, Kakatiyas, Hoysalas, Pandyas).

### 3. Muhammad bin Tughlaq's Ambitious Experiments
1. **Transfer of Capital (1327)**: Moved capital from Delhi to Devagiri (renamed Daulatabad) to protect against Mongol raids and govern South India better. Due to lack of water and harsh travel through heat, the population suffered immensely. Shifted back to Delhi two years later.
2. **Token Currency (1329)**: Introduced bronze/copper coins with same value as silver coins. Did not establish a royal mint monopoly; citizens minted counterfeit coins at home, disrupting trade. Tughlaq had to redeem tokens with genuine gold and silver.
3. **Taxation in Doab**: Increased land revenue in the fertile Ganga-Yamuna Doab during a severe famine, driving peasants to abandon farming.
