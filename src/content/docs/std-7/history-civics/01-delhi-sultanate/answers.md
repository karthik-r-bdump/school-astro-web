## Section A: Multiple Choice Solutions (1 Mark Each)
1. **Question**: Who introduced Sijdah and Paibos?
   - **Answer**: **C) Ghiyas-ud-din Balban** [1 Mark]
2. **Question**: Known as 'Lakhbaksh':
   - **Answer**: **A) Qutb-ud-din Aibak** [1 Mark]
3. **Question**: Malik Kafur was trusted general of:
   - **Answer**: **B) Alauddin Khalji** [1 Mark]

## Section B: Short Answer Solutions (2 Marks Each)
1. **Question**: Opposition to Razia Sultan:
   - **Answer**: Conservative Turkish nobles resented being ruled by a woman who dressed in male attire, appeared unveiled in court, and asserted independent authority. [2 Marks]
2. **Question**: Sijdah and Paibos:
   - **Answer**: *Sijdah* was prostration before the monarch; *Paibos* was kissing the feet of the Sultan to signify absolute subjugation to royal authority. [2 Marks]
3. **Question**: Capital transfer reasons:
   - **Answer**: Daulatabad was centrally located to administer northern and southern territories, and was considered safer from frequent Mongol attacks from the northwest. [2 Marks]
4. **Question**: Dagh and Chehra:
   - **Answer**: *Dagh* was branding royal cavalry horses with an imperial mark to prevent fraud; *Chehra* was keeping a detailed physical descriptive roll of every enlisted soldier. [2 Marks]
5. **Question**: Token currency failure:
   - **Answer**: The state did not maintain a monopoly over minting coins, leading to widespread counterfeiting by goldsmiths and citizens, causing inflation and collapse of foreign trade. [2 Marks]

## Section C: Structured & Long Answer Solutions (3–5 Marks Each)
1. **Question**: Alauddin Khalji's market control:
   - (a) He fixed strict prices for grains, cloth, cattle, and horses, keeping them low so soldiers could maintain high living standards on low cash salaries. [3 Marks]
   - (b) He appointed spies (*Munhiyan*) and market officers (*Shahna-i-Mandi*); merchants caught underweighing goods had flesh of equal weight carved from their bodies. [2 Marks]
2. **Question**: Balban:
   - (a) Kingship: Sultan is the shadow of God (*Zill-i-Ilahi*) and recipient of divine grace (*Niyabat-i-Khudai*), accountable to God alone. [2 Marks]
   - (b) Blood and Iron: Ruthless suppression of rebels, bandits (Mewatis), and defiant nobles through severe punishments and execution. [2 Marks]
   - (c) Chahalgani: He executed or poisoned rebellious members and elevated loyal junior officers to break their factional power. [1 Mark]
