# Chapter 3: The Constitution of India

## 🎯 Syllabus Scope & Objectives
- Definition of Constitution: Fundamental legal document according to which a country is governed.
- The Constituent Assembly: Composition, Dr. Rajendra Prasad (President), Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (Chairman of Drafting Committee).
- Date of adoption (26 November 1949) and date of enforcement (26 January 1950 - Republic Day).
- The Preamble: Key objectives and ideals (Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic, Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity).
- Fundamental Rights: 6 basic rights guaranteed to all citizens.
- Fundamental Duties.

---

## 📖 Key Concepts & Detailed Notes
### 1. Making of the Constitution
- **Constituent Assembly**: Formed in 1946 based on the Cabinet Mission Plan.
  - President of Constituent Assembly: **Dr. Rajendra Prasad**.
  - Chairman of the Drafting Committee: **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** (known as the Father of the Indian Constitution).
- **Key Dates**:
  - Adopted and enacted: **26 November 1949** (celebrated as Constitution Day).
  - Came into full force: **26 January 1950** (celebrated as Republic Day, commemorating the 1930 Purna Swaraj declaration).

### 2. The Preamble: Philosophy of the Constitution
- **Sovereign**: India is completely independent internally and externally, free from any foreign control.
- **Socialist**: Wealth generated socially should be shared equitably; government reduces socio-economic disparities.
- **Secular**: The state has no official religion; all religions are treated with equal respect and freedom.
- **Democratic**: Government is elected by the people through Universal Adult Suffrage.
- **Republic**: The head of the state (President) is elected, not hereditary.

### 3. Fundamental Rights (Part III)
1. **Right to Equality** (Articles 14–18): Equality before law, abolition of untouchability and titles.
2. **Right to Freedom** (Articles 19–22): Freedom of speech, assembly, movement, residence, and profession.
3. **Right against Exploitation** (Articles 23–24): Prohibition of human trafficking, forced labor (begar), and child labor in hazardous occupations.
4. **Right to Freedom of Religion** (Articles 25–28): Freedom to profess, practice, and propagate any religion.
5. **Cultural and Educational Rights** (Articles 29–30): Protection of minority languages, scripts, and educational institutions.
6. **Right to Constitutional Remedies** (Article 32): Right to move the Supreme Court/High Courts for enforcement of rights (called the 'Heart and Soul' of the Constitution by Dr. Ambedkar).
