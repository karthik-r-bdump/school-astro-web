## Section B: Short Answer Solutions (2 Marks Each)
1. **Question**: Define Constitution.
   - **Answer**: A Constitution is the supreme legal document containing the fundamental rules, principles, and laws according to which a country is governed and citizen rights are safeguarded. [2 Marks]
2. **Question**: Significance of 26th January:
   - **Answer**: On 26 January 1930, the Indian National Congress declared *Purna Swaraj* (Complete Independence) at the Lahore session. 26 January 1950 was chosen to commemorate this historic pledge. [2 Marks]
3. **Question**: Democratic vs Republic:
   - **Answer**: A democracy means people elect their government representatives; a republic means the supreme head of state (the President) is elected for a fixed term and does not inherit power through monarchy. [2 Marks]
4. **Question**: Heart and Soul of Constitution:
   - **Answer**: The Right to Constitutional Remedies (Article 32) allows citizens to approach courts directly via writs if any Fundamental Right is violated, making all other rights enforceable and meaningful. [2 Marks]
5. **Question**: Two Fundamental Duties:
   - **Answer**:
     - To abide by the Constitution and respect the National Flag and National Anthem. [1 Mark]
     - To protect and improve the natural environment, including forests, lakes, rivers, and wildlife. [1 Mark]

## Section C: Structured & Long Answer Solutions (3–5 Marks Each)
1. **Question**: Preamble:
   - (a) Sovereign: India is completely independent, free from foreign interference, and supreme in all internal and external matters. [1.5 Marks]
     - Secular: The state has no official religion; treats all faiths with equal dignity and impartiality. [1.5 Marks]
   - (b) Four ideals: **Justice**, **Liberty**, **Equality**, and **Fraternity**. [2 Marks]
2. **Question**: Fundamental Rights:
   - (a) Right against Exploitation prohibits human trafficking, begar (forced unpaid labor), and employment of children under 14 in hazardous factories or mines. [2 Marks]
   - (b) Right to Equality ensures equality before the law, equal protection of laws, abolition of untouchability, and prohibition of discrimination based on religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth. [2 Marks]
   - (c) Yes, Fundamental Rights (except Articles 20 and 21) can be suspended by the President during a National Emergency. [1 Mark]
