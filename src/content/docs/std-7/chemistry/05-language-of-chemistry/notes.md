# Chapter 5: Language of Chemistry

## 🎯 Syllabus Scope & Objectives
- Chemical symbols of common elements.
- Valency: Combining capacity of an atom (concept of electrons lost, gained, or shared).
- Radicals (Polyatomic ions): Monovalent, Divalent, Trivalent.
- Writing chemical formulae using the Criss-Cross method.
- Chemical equations: Word equations and balanced molecular equations.
- Law of Conservation of Mass.

---

## 📖 Key Concepts & Detailed Notes
### 1. Valency of Elements and Radicals
- **Valency**: Number of hydrogen atoms that can combine with or displace one atom of the element.
- **Common Cations (Basic Radicals)**:
  - Monovalent ($+1$): $H^+, Na^+, K^+, NH_4^+, Ag^+$
  - Divalent ($+2$): $Mg^{2+}, Ca^{2+}, Zn^{2+}, Fe^{2+} \text{ (ferrous)}, Cu^{2+} \text{ (cupric)}, Pb^{2+}$
  - Trivalent ($+3$): $Al^{3+}, Fe^{3+} \text{ (ferric)}$
- **Common Anions (Acidic Radicals)**:
  - Monovalent ($-1$): $Cl^-, OH^-, NO_3^-, HCO_3^-$
  - Divalent ($-2$): $O^{2-}, S^{2-}, SO_4^{2-}, CO_3^{2-}$
  - Trivalent ($-3$): $N^{3-}, PO_4^{3-}$

### 2. The Criss-Cross Method
1. Write symbols with positive radical first, negative radical second.
2. Write valencies beneath each symbol.
3. Divide by common factor if any.
4. Criss-cross valencies to form subscripts.
   - Example 1: Aluminum Oxide $\rightarrow Al^{3+} \text{ and } O^{2-} \rightarrow Al_2O_3$.
   - Example 2: Calcium Carbonate $\rightarrow Ca^{2+} \text{ and } CO_3^{2-} \rightarrow CaCO_3$.
   - Example 3: Magnesium Hydroxide $\rightarrow Mg^{2+} \text{ and } OH^- \rightarrow Mg(OH)_2$.
