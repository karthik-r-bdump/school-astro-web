## Section A: Multiple Choice Solutions (1 Mark Each)

   - **Answer**: **B) Nephron**
   - *Marking*: 1 mark for correct option.

2. **Question**: Ultrafiltration takes place in the:
   - **Answer**: **B) Malpighian capsule**

   - **Answer**: **C) Plasma proteins and blood cells**

## Section B: Short Answer Solutions (2 Marks Each)
1. **Question**: Define excretion and name chief waste.
   - **Answer**: Excretion is the biological process of removing toxic metabolic waste products (especially nitrogenous wastes) from the body. [1 Mark] Chief waste: **Urea** [1 Mark].

2. **Question**: Why is the efferent arteriole narrower than the afferent arteriole?
   - **Answer**: The narrower diameter creates high hydrostatic pressure in the glomerulus, which drives ultrafiltration of liquid and dissolved solutes into Bowman's capsule. [2 Marks]

3. **Question**: What is selective reabsorption?
   - **Answer**: The process by which useful substances such as glucose, amino acids, water, and mineral salts from the glomerular filtrate are reabsorbed back into the peritubular capillaries. It occurs primarily in the **Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT)**. [2 Marks]

4. **Question**: Two functions other than excretion:
   - **Answer**:
     - **Osmoregulation**: Regulating water and electrolyte balance. [1 Mark]
     - Regulating blood pH and blood pressure. [1 Mark]

5. **Question**: What is dialysis?
   - **Answer**: A medical procedure that uses an artificial kidney machine to filter metabolic wastes, urea, and excess fluid from blood when a patient experiences kidney failure. [2 Marks]

## Section C: Structured & Long Answer Solutions (3–5 Marks Each)
1. **Question**: Human urinary system:
   - (a) Entering: **Renal Artery**; Leaving: **Renal Vein**. [2 Marks]
   - (b) Pathway: Nephrons $\rightarrow$ Collecting Ducts $\rightarrow$ Renal Pelvis $\rightarrow$ Ureter $\rightarrow$ Urinary Bladder $\rightarrow$ Urethra. [2 Marks]
   - (c) Composition: ~95% water, 2.5% urea, and 2.5% other mineral salts and wastes. [1 Mark]

2. **Question**: Stages of urine formation:
   - (a) Ultrafiltration: Due to high pressure in glomerulus, water and small solutes pass through capillary walls into Bowman's capsule to form glomerular filtrate. [2 Marks]
   - (b) Selective Reabsorption: Cells of PCT actively and passively reabsorb 100% of glucose, amino acids, and ~80% of water and salts back into blood. [2 Marks]
   - (c) Tubular Secretion: Active transport of potassium, hydrogen ions, and drug residues from peritubular capillaries into the distal tubule. [1 Mark]
