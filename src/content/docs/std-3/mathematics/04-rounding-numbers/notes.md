# Chapter 4: Rounding Numbers

## 🎯 What We Will Learn
- Concept of estimation and rounding off.
- The 'Rounding Hill' rule (Digits 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 roll back down; Digits 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 roll forward to the next peak).
- Rounding off to the nearest 10.
- Rounding off to the nearest 100.
- Real-life word problems involving estimation.

---

## 📖 Chapter Notes & Simple Explanations
### 1. The Rounding Hill Adventure
Imagine climbing a hill:
- The base on the left is the lower tens (e.g. 30).
- The peak is in the middle at **5**.
- The base on the right is the upper tens (e.g. 40).
- **Rule of the Hill**:
  - If the digit in the ones place is **0, 1, 2, 3, or 4**: The number rolls back **DOWN** to the lower tens.
  - If the digit in the ones place is **5, 6, 7, 8, or 9**: The number climbs over the peak and rolls **FORWARD** to the higher tens!

### 2. Rounding to the Nearest 10
1. Look at the **Ones** digit.
2. If it is less than 5 ($0, 1, 2, 3, 4$) $\rightarrow$ Round down (ones digit becomes 0, tens digit stays same).
   - Example: $43 \rightarrow 40$.
3. If it is 5 or more ($5, 6, 7, 8, 9$) $\rightarrow$ Round up (ones digit becomes 0, tens digit increases by 1).
   - Example: $47 \rightarrow 50$.
   - Example: $45 \rightarrow 50$.

### 3. Rounding to the Nearest 100
1. Look at the **Tens** digit.
2. If the tens digit is $< 5$ $\rightarrow$ Round down (tens and ones digits become 0).
   - Example: $342 \rightarrow 300$.
3. If the tens digit is $\ge 5$ $\rightarrow$ Round up (hundreds digit increases by 1).
   - Example: $378 \rightarrow 400$.
